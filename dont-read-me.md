# dont read me

My site. Some essays, some fragments, and a room of photographs.

Plain HTML and CSS. No framework, no build step, nothing to install — GitHub Pages serves these files exactly as they sit here.

## Why it looks like this

One rule, repeated: the writing decides. A piece is never set to a template, it is set to what it is.

**Every piece opens on one word.** The first word or two sit alone, larger, before the sentence continues. You have to stop on it.

**One line in a piece is allowed to be louder.** It hangs out into the left margin, so your eye reaches it before you have read your way to it. Most pieces have one. Some have none. Which line it is, is a decision I make by reading the piece, not by counting its words.

**One line is allowed to be quieter** — smaller, grey, set in a little. The thing said under the breath after the thing said out loud.

**A few words are lit in pink.** Read on their own, in order, they say a second thing. They are not links and they are not emphasis. Nobody has to find them.

**The numbers are permanent.** A piece keeps the number it was given, oldest first, so the number is a date and not a ranking. Nothing is ever renumbered because something new arrived.

**Nothing is hidden behind a click.** No tags, no categories, no "read more", no counter telling you how many pieces there are.

## The drawings

They are mine — drawn as SVG paths, not traced from anyone else's work.

Every drawing is a concrete noun taken from that piece's own words. The radio is in *beautiful things* because a song came up on the car radio. The match is in *train station human*. If a piece contains nothing you could draw, it gets nothing.

They float into the column and the text wraps to their actual outline, not to a box around them. They take their colour from the page, so the same drawing is grey in one place and pink in another.

## Some things that are deliberate

*random stuff* is a board, not a list. The notes are shuffled, resized and placed afresh on every visit, each one slightly crooked, because none of them is more important than another. Only the memory at the foot stays put.

The site remembers what you have read, in your own browser. It is never sent anywhere.

`117` sits in a corner of every page. It is not explained.

Not everything here is finished, and that is on purpose.

## Colours

Ink `#111111`, grey `#999999`, pink `#e0218a`, faint pink `#e98cb8`, one red `#b3261e`. Georgia throughout — monospace on exactly one piece, the one that argues with a machine.
