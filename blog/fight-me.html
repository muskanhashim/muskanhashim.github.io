you can't fight me

my mother used to tell me i could either keep my anger or the people in my life, and so i kept swallowing my emotions to make more room for people, until there was barely any room left for myself. keeping the peace with others has many times meant being at war with myself.

its funny how much space that has created - i now feel like a shell of what i used to be. if anger was a bright vibrant red, whatever’s left feels a lot like sitting in an empty beige room - or a train ride you forgot to bring a book for.

i dont know what part of growing up did this but i dont answer in resounding yeses or passionately angry nos. indifference is my answer to everything. i really dont care about lunch or dinner: I’ll happily eat quark for the rest of my life. It’s just that I wish I could go back to the day my father left, so I might remember what it felt like to feel something. His presence probably tensed up my body more than his abandonment but mentally, I aged a hundred years when I saw the hair on my mother’s head turn grey over that one summer he abandoned us
