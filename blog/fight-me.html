you can't fight me

my mother used to tell me i could either keep my anger, even if justified, or the people in my life. it wasn't until my father left that i so completely lost my ego, or any morsel of strength to fight back, that i learned to keep swallowing my emotions to make room for more people, until there was barely any left for myself. keep the peace with others even if it means being at war with yourself.

its funny how much space that created - i now feel like a shell of what i used to be. if anger was a bright vibrant red, whatever’s left feels a lot like sitting in an empty beige room - or a train ride you forgot to bring a book for.

i dont know what part of growing up did this but i dont answer in resounding yeses or passionately angry nos. indifference is my answer to everything. i really dont care about lunch or dinner: I’ll happily eat quark for the rest of my life. It’s just that I wish I could go back to the day my father abandoned us, so I might remember what it felt like to feel something.

- -

His presence probably tensed up my body more than his abandonment but mentally, I aged a hundred years when I saw the hair on my mother’s head turn grey over the course of a few weeks during that one summer he left
